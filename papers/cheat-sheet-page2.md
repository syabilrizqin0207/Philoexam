## Rectification (Lot 9, still open)
- **s 160 LTA**: the court may cancel or correct the register for fraud, omission or mistake.
- **s 160(2)**: never against a registered proprietor in possession unless he was **party or privy** to the fraud, omission or mistake. The price he paid, a late caveat or how recently he bought are irrelevant.
- Mr Ong v Bobby (wilfully blind, so a party to the fraud): rectify. Against an innocent buyer in possession: no.

## Assurance Fund (Lot 9, still open)
- **ss 151, 155 LTA**: pays only for loss caused by the Registrar's or staff's omission, mistake or misfeasance.
- A stranger's forgery alone does not trigger it, and the innocent buyer never pays out of it. **UOB v Bebe**: staff missed a cancelled CT, so the Fund could respond.
- The owner's other routes: sue the forger; caveat at once (**s 115**).

## Option to purchase (Lot 8, still open)
- To the buyer an irrevocable offer: the seller cannot withdraw in the option period (**Spiro v Glencrown**). The contract arises on **exercise**, not on grant.
- Exercise strictly in the manner and time stated (**JT Chanrai**). Option fee about 1%; deposit about 10% (**Linggi**).
- Stamp duty on an exercised OTP: within **14 days**. (You marked this true statement false twice.)

## Express trusts of land (Lot 7, still open)
- A declaration of trust of land needs signed writing (**s 7 Civil Law Act** [check]); resulting and constructive trusts are exempt.
- So an oral "I hold my condo for you" fails as an express trust: argue a resulting or constructive trust instead.

## Unconscionability (Lot 5, still open)
- **BOM v BOK**: the claimant shows an infirmity that the other side exploited; the burden then shifts to that side to prove the deal was fair.
- Not the English "morally reprehensible" test (**Multiservice Bookbinding**), and not "is the lender a bank" or "was the term registered".

## Dispute resolution (Lot 1, still open)
- **Arbitration**: private tribunal; the award binds. **Mediation**: assisted negotiation; binds only once a settlement is signed. A contract dispute is civil, not criminal.

## Agency (Lot 10, checkpoint 20%)
- Authority: actual (express or implied), then apparent: the principal's representation, the third party's reliance, authority he could give (**Freeman & Lockyer**). An agent cannot create his own (**Armagas**).
- Dual agency: acting for both sides and taking both commissions without disclosure and consent breaches fiduciary duty and the CEA rules. The principal recovers the secret commission and refuses commission.
- Bribe: constructive trust (**FHR**), forfeit commission (**Andrews v Ramsay**), rescind, account, damages. Honest discount only: account, keep commission (**Hippisley**).
- Commission: CEA exclusive form, **Clause 6** = any binding sale in validity (up to 3 months, 3-month tail). Non-exclusive = effective cause (**Grandhome**), broken by a time gap, a price change or another agent persuading the buyer.
- Agency ends on death, insolvency or loss of capacity; a registered **Lasting Power of Attorney** survives incapacity.
- Estate Agents Act 2010: agencies licensed, salespersons registered with the CEA; AML due diligence, records kept 5 years.

## Co-ownership script (Lot 7, fixed: keep sharp)
- **s 53(1) LTA**: joint tenants unless TIC stated. Four unities: possession, interest, title, time. TIC needs possession only.
- Actual intention first (**Goh Swee Fang**), then presumptions. The person asserting the RT proves it (**Lau Siew Kim**): practice paper Q1 = D.
- Advancement: parent to child, judged at the transfer (Joy keeps Mdm Wee's flat); none between unmarried partners [check]. Zen's "to have and to hold forever" = immediate gift.
- Severance: declare, serve, **register**; effective on registration (**Chan Lung Kien**). Not a Will, not failed talks (**Burgess v Rawnsley**). By law: bankruptcy, killing (**Re K**), divorce judgment.
- Business partners = TIC in equity (**Malayan Credit**). Sale in lieu: relationship, use, prejudice (**Abu Bakar**). **s 112 Women's Charter**: spouses only.

## Leases (Lot 6, fixed: keep sharp)
- No deed for 7 years or less at a rack rent (**s 53(1) CLPA**); over 7 years must be registered (**s 87 LTA**); a defective lease can be equitable (**Walsh v Lonsdale**). "Writing whatever the length" is false.
- "Until the land is needed for road widening" is void for uncertainty; paid rent gives a yearly tenancy (**Prudential**).
- **OUE v Three Sixty**: the no-set-off clause works, the landlord does not underwrite profits, caveat lessee (no implied fitness for purpose).

## Land, equity, covenants (Lots 1 to 3, fixed: keep sharp)
- Bundle of rights: possess, use, exclude, transfer, and split off (lease, easement, mortgage).
- Fixtures: purpose beats weight (**D'Eyncourt**); a building is land (**Elitestone**) unless custom (**Kiah**); tenant removes during the term or soon after; a fixed hire-purchase item goes with the land (**Hobson v Gorringe**).
- Airspace: a low sign is trespass (**Kelsen**); an aircraft at a reasonable height is not (**Bernstein**).
- Conversion: a buyer under a good contract owns in equity; the seller is a bare trustee (**Lysaght v Edwards**).
- **s 47(2) LTA**: knowing of an unregistered interest is not fraud; caveat it or prove dishonesty.
- Clean hands: no specific performance for a buyer who refused, then sued when prices rose (**Lee Christina**).
- The burden of any covenant never runs at law (**Austerberry**; **Rhone v Stephens**).

## Easements, caveats, ABSD (Lots 4 and 9, fixed: keep sharp)

- Caveats: interest existing at lodgment (**s 115**; **Asiatic**); no bare money claim (**Salbiah**); lapse 5 years (**s 121**); wrongful caveat compensation (**s 128**); removal **s 127(1)** or **(2)**.
